This dataset contains surface electromyography (sEMG) signals recorded from twenty healthy subjects performing five hand gestures.
The data were collected from three EMG channels placed on the dominant forearm (anterior forearm, posterior forearm, and anterior radius), with a sampling rate of 1000 Hz.

The five gestures performed were:

1.- Bending of all fingers in a fist gesture

2.- Bending of the thumb

3.- Rest position

4.- Extension of the five fingers

5.- Bending of the middle and ring fingers

Each subject performed the gestures with an average force, completing 20 repetitions per gesture (a total of 100 samples per subject).

The dataset was acquired using a battery-powered acquisition system, which minimized electrical noise.
The signals were only analog filtered, allowing users to apply their own digital processing or feature extraction algorithms depending on the application.

The dataset includes raw and pre-processed EMG signals, intended for training and evaluating artificial intelligence algorithms.
It can be used for developing or benchmarking models that require a large dataset for accurate gesture and force classification in EMG-based systems.

Dataset Structure
The dataset contains seven .mat files and twenty .tdms files, organized as follows:

EMG_DB:
Raw EMG signals from the 20 subjects, containing 2000 samples × 3 channels × 4500 data points.
Every 400 samples correspond to one gesture, in ascending order from gesture 1 (fist) to gesture 5 (rest).

EMG_S:
EMG signals with window segmentation, each window having a 200 ms length and 80% overlap.
This file contains 174,000 windows × 3 channels × 200 data points.

Mov1 – Mov5:
Raw signals of each gesture acquisition, each containing 400 samples × 3 channels × 4500 data points.

-Mov1: first gesture acquisition

-Mov2: second gesture acquisition

-Mov3: third gesture acquisition

-Mov4: fourth gesture acquisition

-Mov5: fifth gesture acquisition

Each .tdms file contains the raw data of one subject’s gestures and indicates the ranges corresponding to correctly performed samples.
